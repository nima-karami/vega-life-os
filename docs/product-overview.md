# VEGA · Life OS — what it is

A personal command center for one person's life. It holds what you care about,
what you're working toward, what you do every day, and what you spend — in one
place, connected, so the day in front of you can be traced back to a reason.

It is a private tool, not a team product. There is no sharing, no social layer,
no feed of other people. One operator, one life.

## The core idea

Most productivity tools start at the task and stop there. You end up with a tidy
list and no answer to "why am I doing any of this?"

VEGA starts at the other end. Everything ladders up:

**Values → Goals → Habits → Tasks**

- A **value** is something you've decided matters — health, craft, family. It's the
  root, and it owns a colour that everything beneath it wears, so you can see at a
  glance which part of your life a given day is serving.
- A **goal** is a finite thing you're trying to reach under one of those values. It
  has progress and a pace: are you ahead of where you'd need to be, or behind?
- A **habit** is the recurring behaviour that keeps a value alive whether or not
  there's a goal attached. Measured by consistency, not completion.
- A **task** is one thing to do. It inherits its meaning from whatever it hangs off.

Nothing has to be tagged with importance by hand. Where something sits in the
ladder is what makes it matter, and the app derives the rest from that.

## What you actually do with it

### Today

The default screen. Your day: what's scheduled, which habits are due, what's left
to do. When it's all done, it says so and gets out of the way. This is the only
screen you need on an ordinary morning.

### Calendar

A week grid you can drag things onto. Tasks and habit slots become real blocks of
time, and it syncs both ways with Google Calendar, so the rest of your life shows
up next to your intentions instead of in a separate app. A focus filter narrows
the week to one value or goal when you want to see only that.

### Values, goals and habits

Each one gets its own full page, not a cramped dialog. A value page shows the
goals and habits living under it. A goal page shows its steps and whether the pace
is holding. A habit page shows the streak, the schedule, and the record of days
kept and missed. Habits can be active, paused, or archived — pausing is for life
happening, and it doesn't punish you with a broken streak you didn't earn.

### Journal and reflection

A daily entry: a mood, a few things you're grateful for, and space to write. The
gratitude step comes first, deliberately, before the page opens up. Past entries
are browsable, and missed days get a gentle nudge to fill in rather than a scold.

### Weekly review

A once-a-week mirror. How much did you complete, how did your mood run against
your own four-week baseline, which habits held and which slipped, which values got
attention and which got none. Then guided prompts to write about it.

### Analytics

Six views over any time window: mood, reflection, day-by-day consistency, balance
across values, habit performance, goal pace. Not vanity charts — each one is meant
to answer a question you'd actually ask about yourself.

### BrainWrite

A personal deck of cards — notes, quotes and prompts you've collected, including
vocabulary you're learning. You review them by swiping: the ones you know drift
further out, the ones you don't come back sooner. It's the part of the app for
things you want to keep in your head rather than in a list.

### Finance

A month-by-month ledger. Import statements from a file, sort transactions into
your own categories, watch spending against what you meant to spend. It handles
shared expenses two-sided, so a month can be closed out and settled with another
person and the balance carries forward cleanly.

### The command bar

⌘K from anywhere. Type what you want in plain language — add a task, complete
something, skip a habit, plan the week, jump to a page — instead of hunting for
the screen that does it.

## Principles it's built on

**Your data is yours.** Signed out, it runs entirely on your own machine as a
sandbox with sample data you can wipe. Signed in, it's yours on the server and
nowhere else. Nothing is sold, shared, or mined.

**Calm over loud.** No badges screaming at you, no streak guilt, no gamified
confetti. The interface is quiet and mostly monochrome; colour is reserved for
meaning — which value something belongs to — and nothing else.

**One thing per screen.** Each page has a single job and one obvious focus. If a
page is trying to do two things, that's a defect.

**Honest measurement.** A day you deliberately skipped isn't a day you failed.
Paused habits don't rot. Missing data reads as missing, not as zero. The numbers
are only useful if you trust them enough not to game them.

**Works on a phone.** Not a shrunken desktop app — the phone layout is a first
citizen, because a day gets logged while you're standing up, not at a desk.

## Who it's for

One person who wants their days to line up with what they say they care about, and
who'd rather see that connection on a screen than hope it's true. It assumes you
already know what you value; it doesn't try to coach you into a philosophy. Its job
is to keep the ladder visible and make the daily act of climbing it cheap.
